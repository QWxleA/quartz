> [!NOTE] compound knowledge explained  
> Compound knowledge means combining old and new insights into richer, more developed ideas.

**Compound knowledge** in the context of Zettelkasten refers to the way knowledge grows and evolves by *combining* older insights with newer ones through ongoing linking and interaction. It’s similar to the idea of *compound interest* in finance—just as money grows over time through ==repeated accumulation of interest==, knowledge deepens and expands when you ==continuously revisit and connect ideas.==

In practice, this means:
- You write a new note and link it to an older one.
- That older idea gets *recontextualized* by the new connection, possibly gaining new meaning. → [[context is everything]]
- Over time, multiple links to and from that note may surface connections you hadn’t planned. 
- This process leads to richer, more nuanced thinking, where ideas aren't static but constantly shaped by new input. → [[links are more valuable than the notes they connect]]

AI supplied example:  
> You might write an early note on *confirmation bias*. Months later, you create a note on *scientific skepticism* and link it to the first. In doing so, both ideas evolve—*confirmation bias* now contributes to your understanding of *skepticism*, and vice versa. Each new connection is a compound layer added to your knowledge network.

This ==dynamic layering of thoughts== is one of the core strengths of the Zettelkasten method.
