A **zettelkasten** (German for "slip box") is a knowledge management method where you create a network of interconnected, atomic notes that build understanding through **emergent connections** rather than rigid hierarchical organization.

See: [[Zettelkasten and Evergreen MOC]] for implementation and best practices

## Classical zettelkasten, the Luhmann show
This system implemented by [[Niklas Luhmann]], consisted of ==two slip boxes==, one for the biographical notes, the other for his ideas

> [!example] Box 1 - **Bibliography**
> - title, isbn etc
> - brief notes about content

> [!example] Box 2 - **NL's ideas and thoughts on the subject**
> - Short and brief, but full sentences
> - Note written in context of other notes, linking ideas, _not_ a rehashing of the source material
> - No quoting, strive original wording

Example: [[English Translation of All Notes on Zettelkasten by Luhmann]]

>[!note] Four key identifiers of NL's ZK
> 1. **System of organization:** sections, then card numbers
> 	- **Placement**: where in the slip-box a note was located indicated the primary context of the note [[folgezettel]]
> 	- **Index cards**: Index as _entry point_ to a group of notes (chain of thought)
> 2. **Numbering system**: numbers and letters
> 3. **Linking system**
> 	- **Links**: Linked note to more than one other note gave it several more contexts
> 4. **Keyword index**


## More modern implementation by Ahrens
[[Sönke Ahrens]]  _named_ the different kind of notes, and (slightly) formalized the approach:
- [[Fleeting notes]] — temporary, stream of conscience initial notes. Located in the inbox, and will be discarded when processed
- [[atomic note|Permanent notes]] — permanent as in never thrown away, not that they can't be edited. Stored as literature notes in the reference system or in the slip-box.
- [[project|Project notes]] — the same approach as the former, but within a limited ==scope== of a project. Upon completion discarded or archived.

## (Primary) Sources
At its simplest, a zettelkasten is a [[commonplace book]] with an index, links and condensed notes of specific ideas we wrote ourselves [[Reframing and simplifying the idea of how to keep a Zettelkasten - Chris Aldrich]]

> A Zettelkasten is a personal tool for thinking and writing. It has hypertextual features to make a web of thought possible. The difference to other systems is that you create a web of thoughts instead of notes of arbitrary size and form, and emphasize connection, not a collection. – [Introduction to the Zettelkasten Method • Zettelkasten Method](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/)
